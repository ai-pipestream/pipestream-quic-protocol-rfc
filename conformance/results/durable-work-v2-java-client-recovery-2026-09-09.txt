1. Section 12.1 refused-stream rule measured as stream credit on both stacks (CR14)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
New RawDurablePeer.streamCredit()/awaitStreamCredit(): the peer's remaining
unidirectional stream allowance (Netty QuicChannel.peerAllowedStreams, read on
the connection's event loop), i.e. QUIC MAX_STREAMS credit minus open streams.

Rust authority: RawPeerRustAuthorityTest.refusedInputsReturnStreamCreditOnTheRustAuthority
(sealed-interop). Rust default offer selects stream-limit 4. Twelve inputs
(three times the allowance) are refused alternately as truncated-with-FIN
(digest/length contradiction) and reset-after-partial-payload; each refusal is
correlated to its stream with INTEGRITY_ERROR and a named detail; after each
one the allowance is back at 4 with zero wait; the connection stays open; a
valid transfer then admits and reaches SUCCEEDED; DETACH acknowledged; the
server drains on SIGTERM with DRAINED. Observations (written by the test):
  raw/rust-stream-credit-observations-2026-09-09.tsv
  ordinal shape                stream code             detail                    before at-refusal after wait-ms
  0       truncated-fin        2      INTEGRITY_ERROR  truncated payload         4      4          4     0
  1       reset-after-partial  6      INTEGRITY_ERROR  input stream interrupted  4      4          4     0
  ...     (12 rows, every one 4/4/4/0)
  Test log: raw/rust-stream-credit-2026-09-09.log (Tests run: 1, Failures: 0).

Java authority: DurableWireNegativeTest.sequentialInputsBeyondTheConcurrentLimitReplenishStreamCredit
now asserts peer.awaitStreamCredit(2) right after every refused input (stream
limit 2, seven inputs, every third one corrupt) before the next stream is
opened. Green on pipestream.4 (raw/wire-credit-observation-2026-09-09.log,
6/6). On the .3 transport this assertion cannot hold: quiche never collected
a locally stopped peer-unidirectional stream (see
durable-work-v2-java-drained-streams-2026-09-09.txt).

Both stacks are therefore held to the identical observation the amended CR14
row requires. Reading of the numbers: QUIC MAX_STREAMS is a cumulative limit
and RFC 9000 Section 4.6 leaves its advancement policy to implementations;
the Section 12.1 rule is PipeStream's own requirement that refused streams
advance it. The normative observation is that replacement streams keep
opening beyond the initial allowance (twelve refusals against an allowance of
four, then a valid transfer). "Back at 4 after each refusal with 0 ms wait"
is fixture evidence about these two implementations, not a QUIC rule; an
implementation that batched its MAX_STREAMS advances could satisfy the rule
without matching that value.

2. Local diagnostics for the bound that ended a transfer (D1, Section 12.1)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
ProtocolError now keeps its bounded detail (detail()) and whether the peer
authority named it (fromAuthority(); ProtocolError.refused(code, detail) is
used for correlated REFUSALs and coded application closes). DurableServer
puts the local label into the REFUSAL detail (respondRefusal and
sendInputRefusal: e.g. "input receive deadline", "owner connection ceiling",
"truncated payload") instead of the constants "refused"/"input refused", the
same practice as the Rust authority (quinn/src/v2_authority/input.rs puts
error.detail into Refusal.detail). The detail is capped at 120 characters,
never parsed by the client, and carries no state.
V2Main prints "REFUSED code=<CODE> detail=<peer text>" on stdout for any
authority refusal (local failures reach stderr only; every failure exits 1),
and the new "client capabilities" operation prints the offered and selected
stream idle/lifetime deadlines and the selected limits.
Checked by V2MainProcessTest (a stale expected-attempt retry from the launcher
process prints REFUSED code=...; a local INTEGRITY_ERROR does not) and
ClientRecoveryTest (CAPABILITIES line).

3. Client recovery modes (D2/D3, Section 12.2.1, CR01/CR09)
-----------------------------------------------------------
ClientCommands: --retry-budget N (default 0 = one-shot) and
--retry-backoff-ms M (default 200; doubling, capped at 30 s, plus jitter).
Recoverable: authority LIMIT_EXCEEDED/NOT_READY/WAIT_TIMEOUT/INTERNAL_ERROR,
CONTROL_RESET, IOException (transport loss), TimeoutException (local request
deadline). Never: local validation, journal (SQLException) failures, CONFLICT,
UNAUTHORIZED, framing/integrity codes, terminal outcomes. Every attempt is a
new connection with fresh request numbers; the journal supplies the original
identity and parameters (journalMutation/journalInput are idempotent for equal
requests and CONFLICT for different ones). Output: RECOVERING attempt=..
budget=.. code=.. wait-ms=.. per retry; UNRESOLVED attempts=.. last=..
budget-exhausted|not-recoverable then the last failure (exit 1). The intent
stays journaled and "unresolved" lists it; no outcome is manufactured.

ClientRecoveryTest (2 tests, in-process, through ClientCommands.run with
captured stdout; raw/client-recovery-2026-09-09.log):
  budgetedInvocationRecoversFromPreAdmissionCapacityRefusals
    Real pre-commit refusal: a second owner parks the only storage worker
    inside a declaration commit (test hook) and fills the two-deep queue, so
    the authority answers LIMIT_EXCEEDED before any commit. The budgeted
    "admit" (budget 12, backoff 100 ms) is refused at least twice on fresh
    connections (RECOVERING ... code=LIMIT_EXCEEDED), the hook releases the
    worker after the second refusal, the next attempt admits: exactly one
    RECEIPT, exactly one ADMISSION_COMMITTED, journal receipt Admitted attempt
    1, nothing unresolved; the other owner's queued declarations complete.
  lostRepliesAreRecoveredByReplayInBothModesAndBudgetExhaustionStaysUnresolved
    Real reply loss after a real commit (withhold ADMISSION_RESPONSE_SENT):
    with every reply withheld, budget 2 gives three attempts (commit, replay,
    replay), prints "UNRESOLVED attempts=3 last=CONTROL_RESET
    budget-exhausted", exits with CONTROL_RESET, one ADMISSION_COMMITTED, the
    operation listed by "unresolved" with no receipt. Then the one-shot
    "replay" invocation (with a budget of 5 while two more replies are
    withheld) recovers the same operation: 2 RECOVERING lines, 1 RECEIPT,
    still one commit; a one-shot "lookup" resolves it with no retry; the
    journal shows Admitted attempt 1 and nothing unresolved. A CONFLICT
    (changed parameters under the reused identity) stops at once even with a
    budget ("UNRESOLVED attempts=1 last=CONFLICT not-recoverable").
    Finally "capabilities" prints the offered/selected deadlines.

The test plan now states the CR01 recovery mode (driver re-invocation of the
one-shot client, plus the client's own budget option where it exists); both
modes are exercised above. CR09 is covered for CONTROL_RESET; disconnect and
process death remain covered by DurableLifecycleTest/FixtureMainTest.

4. Lookup during a pending admission (CR04)
-------------------------------------------
ClientRecoveryTest.lookupDuringAPendingAdmissionReportsAbsenceAndTheOriginalIdentityCommitsOnce
(raw/client-recovery-cr04-2026-09-09.log). The library client journals and
transmits an admission; the authority installs its input and a test hook
parks the storage worker between INPUT_INSTALLED and the admission commit,
so the request is genuinely pending and uncommitted (ADMISSION_COMMITTED
count 0). A second connection of the same owner attaches to the session and
asks LookupOperation for that identity: NOT_FOUND. The library client itself
refuses to look up an identity it never journaled, locally, without sending
(NOT_FOUND, not from the authority): a new identity is never inferred from
absence. The hook releases; the original completes with Admitted attempt 1;
the second connection's lookup now returns the identical receipt. Then the
launcher's one-shot "replay" of the same journaled identity returns the
retained receipt with no second commit (count stays 1), "lookup" agrees, and
the journal holds one receipt and nothing unresolved.

5. Local journal I/O failures (CR10)
------------------------------------
ClientJournalFaultTest.journalFaultsAreSurfacedAtEveryPersistencePointAndResolvedByTheNextInvocation
(raw/client-journal-fault-2026-09-09.log). The client journal runs in
SQLite rollback-journal mode, so a read-only journal directory makes the
next write transaction fail with a real SQLITE_CANTOPEN from the VFS, as an
unwritable disk would; no mock and no process kill. Three injection points,
each through the launcher with client boundary hooks recording what happened:
  (a) before transmission: the admission intent cannot be journaled ->
      local INTERNAL_ERROR ("client storage failed"), not from the authority,
      "UNRESOLVED attempts=1 last=INTERNAL_ERROR not-recoverable" even with a
      budget; no INTENT_JOURNALED and no REQUEST_SENT for that operation (the
      session attach was sent; it is not a mutation); zero commits at the
      authority; after the directory is writable again the journal holds no
      trace of the operation.
  (b) while saving the received receipt: the fault is armed at the client's
      own INTENT_JOURNALED boundary for the declaration, so the intent is
      journaled and sent, the authority commits, RECEIPT_VALIDATED fires, and
      journaling the receipt fails -> local INTERNAL_ERROR, no RECEIPT line,
      no RECEIPT_JOURNALED, the operation listed by "unresolved" with no
      receipt. Writable again, the one-shot "replay" resolves the same
      identity from retained state (RECEIPT), and "page --scope 0" shows
      exactly the two declared entities (one effect). Note: the authority
      reports DECLARATION_COMMITTED for the replay's idempotent transaction
      too, so that boundary counts transactions, not effects, for declares.
  (c) while saving a verified output selection: after a completed admission
      and a retained manifest, "select" fails locally (INTERNAL_ERROR) while
      the journal is unwritable; writable again, "select" succeeds and "read"
      installs the digest-verified output (VERIFIED, bytes identical).

6. Temporary authorization withdrawal, unsafe clock, durable revocation (CR06)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AuthorizationClockRecoveryTest.temporaryWithdrawalUnsafeClockAndDurableRevocationAreDistinctAndNeverRetried
(raw/authorization-clock-recovery-2026-09-09.log). The host's OwnerPolicy
reads a live principal map and the host clock is a test-controlled UtcClock.
  (a) withdrawal: alice is removed from the owner policy while her credential
      still maps; the launcher's admission is refused UNAUTHORIZED by the
      authority; "--retry-budget 3" makes no retry ("UNRESOLVED attempts=1
      last=UNAUTHORIZED not-recoverable"); a lookup of an earlier receipt on a
      fresh connection is refused too (reconnecting with a cached receipt
      grants nothing); no admission effect. Restored, the same journaled
      identity commits once (Admitted attempt 1, nothing unresolved).
  (b) unsafe clock: with the clock untrusted, the admission is refused
      CLOCK_UNSAFE from the authority and never retried; result delivery is
      refused CLOCK_UNSAFE as well (retention is evaluated against UTC; no
      output file is left behind), while lookup, watch and manifest keep
      working under current authorization. A clock one hour behind the
      durable watermark is refused the same way ("UTC precedes durable
      watermark"). Trusted again, the same journaled admission commits once
      and the read installs the verified output.
  (c) durable revocation: host.revoke(generation) with the owner fully
      authorized and the clock trusted leaves every session-scoped request
      UNAUTHORIZED (lookup, admission), with no retry and no restoration.
      Temporary authorization failure and durable session revocation are
      thus distinct at the client: the first is resolved by the next
      invocation after legitimate restoration, the second is not.
