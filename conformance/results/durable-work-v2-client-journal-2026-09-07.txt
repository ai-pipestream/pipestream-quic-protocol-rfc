Scope
  v2::client::Journal persists one configured creation/session and the six
  immutable mutation kinds before transmission. It uses real SQLite FULL
  synchronous transactions, the existing guarded physical backend, independent
  client application/format identifiers and checksummed identity-bound images.
  No normative wire/CDDL, authority storage format or dependency version changed.
  No JSON, new language implementation or opaque serialized server snapshot.

  The expected authority/owner, creation sequence, exact policy, selected durable
  profile combination and quotas must match on reopen. Missing, empty, corrupt
  or incompatible history fails closed. Matching prepare/receipt replay is
  idempotent; changed immutable content is refused. Stored request numbers are
  normalized and fresh wire numbers are supplied by the current connection.

  The default operation ceiling is 4096, configurable through 1,000,000. Images
  are length-checked before allocation and decoded with canonical typed codecs.
  Unresolved pages contain at most 256 records; cursors never use SQLite's random
  row-ID fallback at integer exhaustion. Inventory is never automatically evicted.
  Opening audits retained records one at a time; preparation checks the retained
  inventory. These blocking calls belong outside a transport control reader.
  File-length limits do not imply measured heap/RSS/native-memory limits.

  Receipt checks bind the operation digest and typed outcome to original intent.
  They do not authenticate a caller-supplied record or verify full scope coverage.
  Missing receipt, failed local write, NOT_FOUND, refusal or transport loss never
  establishes non-commit or authorizes a replacement work/operation/attempt.

  The forced-exit test starts another process, waits for its post-commit marker,
  kills it, observes unsuccessful exit and exclusively reopens real history.
  The exact unresolved operation remains. The physical-exhaustion test uses
  actual 64 KiB database/WAL limits, verifies refusal without saving new intent,
  checks measured file lengths and reopens the previously committed intent.
  A failing SQLite receipt-write trigger tests atomic rollback and preserved
  uncertainty; it does not change a production refusal or weaken a test oracle.

Two real-QUIC tests with exclusive client journal reopen
  journal_replays_unrecorded_creation_and_declaration_without_new_identities
    Receive but do not save creation and declaration replies. Reopen, reconnect
    with rotated owner credentials, replay the same creation and operation,
    validate/store the identical binding and receipt, and confirm next sequence 2.
  journal_recovers_unrecorded_input_admission_and_reads_the_original_attempt_output
    Persist declaration receipt before input. Persist the exact input descriptor
    and operation before sending 64 KiB. Do not save the admission reply; reopen
    and attach with rotated credentials, recover via operation lookup, then read
    the original attempt-1 output with verified bytes.
  These tests share the Rust wire codec and authenticated listener. They do not
  kill the authority or provide the independent cross-language failure oracle.
